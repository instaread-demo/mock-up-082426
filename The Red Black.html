    DAWGFEST Vintage Market returns: “A celebration of everything Athens”

    • Carly Diver

      Carly Diver

    • Aug 23, 2026 Updated 8 hrs ago

    The DAWGFEST Vintage Market is an event held twice a year at the beginning and end of the academic year in Athens, offering an array of hidden gems. The event ran from noon to 4 p.m. on Sunday, Aug. 23 at the Classic Center Pavilion. 

    “DAWGFEST is a celebration of everything Athens. It's celebrating vintage, and then all things Georgia-related,” said Davis Parker, the executive producer of DAWGFEST. “We have jewelry, we have vintage clothes, we have plants, we have all types of things and it's kind of just a celebration of school being back.”

    The whole point of DAWGFEST, according to Parker, is about getting a “local-Athens-buzz,” with an event anyone can participate in.

    “This is the best time of the year. It's back to school, there's so much going on, and this is something that's during the day that really you can do with anyone,” Parker said.

    Parker began hosting DAWGFEST, and other similar events, in 2021 following the COVID-19 pandemic.

    “The vintage scene was kind of exploding, and we thought Athens definitely deserved to be a part of that," Parker said.

    Countless vendors set up their stations with clothing racks, clothing piles, jewelry displays, hat stands and more, but some patrons didn’t attend solely for the shopping. 

    For Shania Bautista, a sophomore majoring in bioengineering and pre-med at the University of Georgia, seeing others’ creative expression is a large factor in why she attended. This was Bautista’s third time at DAWGFEST. 

    “I think just the vibes are cool. I like looking at random stuff. I like seeing what other people are getting,” Bautista said. “This is like a fun free—not free if you, you know, [spend money] — it's a fun activity to go and do with your friends.”

    Athenians and UGA students alike are no strangers to limited-time, in-person events curated for them. Occasions like DAWGFEST present opportunities for people to explore, socialize and make memories.  

    “It got put in our group chat,” said Sophie Arscott, a UGA sophomore and hospitality major. “This was my first time attending…I really like it, I love seeing everyone's outfits, everyone looks so cute…it feels like a little community.”

    With heavy foot traffic throughout the afternoon, the event drew a major turn-out. While scavenging for their next hidden gems, patrons were bustling by one another sporting individual styles. 

    “I think recently, whenever I go to the store, I see the same clothes over on repeat, and it kind of irritates me a little bit because I feel like my outfits can't be unique,” Arscott said. “But when I go thrifting or [shopping for] vintage, I can find pieces that fit more of my style and that are more curated to me.”

    According to Bautista, this event is a great opportunity to be exposed to new fashions and clothing options. 

    “I just feel like college students love dressing up…this is the time where so many people are trying to find themselves, their own personalities and their own styles and everything," Bautista said.

    Parker said he wants patrons to start the year off strong with a fun event. 

    “I hope they just have a good day in the Georgia heat, and that they run into a buddy or run into a friend, that they find a really cool t-shirt or a really cool game day outfit, that they just, you know, kind of have a good time in the beginning of the year,” Parker said.
